THE SQL SCALE-UP
Retail Analytics

Setup

The retail client is blunt: “We can’t keep re-running a spreadsheet every quarter. We need this segmentation living in BigQuery, refreshing automatically, feeding straight into our marketing tools.”

Evidence

  • The same RFM logic from the spreadsheet, but now against a full retail sales dataset in BigQuery.
  • 5 bands per metric instead of 3, producing 125 permutations grouped into 11 named DMA segments.
  • Frequency is computed as a rate (purchases ÷ months active), not a raw count — normalizing for customer tenure.

The Mystery

With 5 bands each for R, F, and M, there are 125 possible score combinations. How do you make that actionable?

Make Your Move

Choose your first move before continuing. There’s no need to know the answer yet — tap an option to see why it’s worth considering.

Why this move

F and M are combined into a single fm_score (since both indicate purchase volume), reducing 125 permutations to a 5×5 = 25 grid. The DMA’s 11-segment model maps every cell of that grid to a named segment with a specific marketing action.

Real-World Use Cases

Production-grade segmentationA spreadsheet works for 2,000 customers; BigQuery handles millions. The SQL pipeline refreshes automatically and feeds directly into marketing tools.
Quintile-based scoringAPPROX_QUANTILES adapts to any data distribution — no hard-coded thresholds. The data defines the bands, making the model portable across industries.
DMA 11-segment standardThe UK Data & Marketing Association’s segment definitions are the industry benchmark — knowing them is expected in product analytics interviews.
Recency inversion trapLow recency days = high score (recent buyer). Getting this backwards flips every segment. It’s a classic interview gotcha and a real production bug.
